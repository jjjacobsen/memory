What did C S Lewis mean by mere Christianity?|The core Christian belief shared by nearly all Christians across time, not the disputed details that separate denominations
Why does Lewis avoid denominational disputes in Mere Christianity?|He wants to help outsiders understand the shared Christian faith before discussing disputes that belong inside the family of believers
How does Lewis compare mere Christianity to a hall and rooms?|The hall is shared Christian belief, while the rooms are particular churches where people worship and obey
What warning does Lewis give about staying in the hall?|The hall is a waiting place, not a home, so a believer should seek a room while still respecting people in other rooms
What ordinary human behavior starts Lewis argument for the moral law?|Quarreling, because people appeal to a standard of fairness they expect others to recognize
Why is the moral law different from a law of nature like gravity?|A physical law describes what things do, while the moral law tells people what they ought to do and can disobey
What does Lewis mean by the law of human nature?|A real standard of right and wrong that humans recognize and use when judging conduct
Why does moral disagreement not disprove the moral law?|Disagreement usually assumes a shared standard, just as arguing about a calculation assumes arithmetic
Why is the moral law not just herd instinct?|It judges between instincts, telling us which impulse should be encouraged or restrained
Why is the moral law not just social convention?|We can judge one society as morally better or worse than another, which requires a standard beyond custom
What two facts form the starting point of Lewis argument in Book I?|Humans know the moral law, and humans fail to keep it
Why does Lewis say the moral law is more than a fact about what people actually do?|People often do the opposite of it, yet still feel they ought to have obeyed it
What does the moral law reveal about the universe in Lewis argument?|It suggests that reality contains something more like a mind or will than mere matter
Why can science by itself not answer what lies behind the universe?|Science studies observed facts inside the universe, not the ultimate meaning or source behind all facts
Why does Lewis say looking within ourselves gives a clue to what is behind the universe?|We experience ourselves as agents under a moral demand, which is information from inside reality rather than only external observation
What is the first thing Christianity says humans need to face?|The moral law is real, and we have broken it
Why does Lewis say a comforting God is not the first Christian discovery?|A truly good God is also a danger to people who are morally guilty
Why does Christianity make sense only after the bad news of moral failure?|The offer of forgiveness answers a real problem only if guilt before the moral law is real
What mistake does Lewis think people make when they want religion only for comfort?|They want reassurance without first facing truth, repentance, and the seriousness of good
What are the two main views of God Lewis contrasts at the start of Book II?|Pantheism sees God as beyond good and evil or identical with the universe, while Christianity sees God as good and distinct from creation
Why does Lewis think atheism is too simple?|It condemns the world as unjust, but that judgment depends on a real standard of justice
Why does Lewis reject the idea that evil is equal and opposite to good?|Evil is parasitic on good, because badness depends on twisting good things like intelligence, will, and desire
What is dualism in Lewis discussion?|The belief that good and evil are two independent powers of equal status
Why can there be badness without a bad God, but not badness without goodness?|Badness is spoiled goodness, so it needs good things to corrupt
Why does Lewis call Christianity the story of an invasion?|It presents the world as enemy occupied territory where God has landed to reclaim it
What role does free will play in Lewis explanation of evil?|Free will makes love and joy possible, but also makes rebellion possible
Why did God create free creatures despite the risk of evil?|A world of automata could not love, obey freely, or know real happiness
What is the central choice Lewis says people face about Jesus?|Jesus must be rejected or received as who he claimed to be, not reduced to a merely good moral teacher
Why does Lewis think Jesus cannot be treated as only a great human teacher?|His claims to forgive sins and speak with divine authority would be intolerable unless true
What does Lewis mean by the shocking alternative?|Jesus is either radically wrong about himself or truly God, so neutrality toward his claims does not work
What problem does the Incarnation address in Lewis argument?|God enters human life so that divine life can be joined to human nature from within
What does Lewis say Christians are not required to have about the atonement?|A single complete theory explaining exactly how the death of Christ works
What does Lewis compare theories of the atonement to?|Maps or models that may help explain reality but are not the thing itself
Why can Christ perform perfect repentance in Lewis argument?|As God he has perfect goodness, and as man he can submit, suffer, and die
What does Lewis mean by repentance as a kind of death?|Repentance means laying down pride and self will, not merely feeling sorry
Why do humans need God to help them repent?|The bad person needs repentance, but only a good person can repent perfectly
What practical ways does Lewis name for receiving the Christ life?|Baptism, belief, and the Eucharist
Why does Lewis describe Christianity as more than moral advice?|It is the transmission of a new kind of life, not just instruction in better behavior
Why does Lewis compare Christians to organs in a body?|Christ works through the whole Church, with individual believers sharing one life while remaining distinct
What is the practical conclusion of Book II?|God has acted in Christ, and people are invited to receive and live from the new life he gives
What are the three parts of morality in Lewis framework?|Fair play between people, harmony within each person, and the proper purpose of human life as a whole
What ship analogy does Lewis use for morality?|A fleet needs ships that do not collide, ships that are seaworthy inside, and a correct destination
Why is inner character part of morality, not only outward behavior?|Bad inner condition eventually damages actions and relationships, and the person is meant for a final purpose
What are the cardinal virtues Lewis discusses?|Prudence, temperance, justice, and fortitude
What is prudence in Lewis account?|Practical good sense that thinks clearly about what one is doing and what will result
What is temperance in Lewis account?|Right ordering of pleasures, not necessarily total abstinence from every pleasure
What is justice in Lewis account?|Fairness, honesty, truthfulness, keeping promises, and giving others their due
What is fortitude in Lewis account?|Courage that faces danger and endurance that continues under pain
Why are virtues more than isolated right actions?|A virtue is a trained quality of character that shapes future choices
What does Lewis say Christianity contributes to social morality?|It gives principles such as love, justice, and generosity, while detailed programs require human skill and work
Why does Lewis say Christian giving should sometimes hurt?|Generosity should be real enough to limit personal comfort, not only dispose of leftovers
What mistake does Lewis warn against in applying Christian social teaching?|Using Christianity as a substitute for doing the hard practical work of politics, economics, and justice
How does Lewis distinguish psychoanalysis from morality?|Psychoanalysis may heal disordered feelings, while morality concerns what a person chooses to do with the material available
Why should humans be cautious in judging other people morally?|People begin with very different temperaments, wounds, and pressures, while God sees the full raw material
What does Lewis mean by the raw material of a person?|The inherited temperament, upbringing, and psychological conditions from which choices are made
What is chastity in Lewis account?|Sexual obedience according to Christian teaching, not merely following the changing standards of social propriety
Why does Lewis say chastity is difficult but still required?|A command can be hard without being unreal, and repeated failure does not remove the obligation to keep trying
What practical warning does Lewis give about sexual appetite?|Modern culture can inflame desire while pretending the appetite is ordinary and harmless in any use
How does Lewis define Christian marriage?|A lifelong union in which two people become one organism, not merely a temporary partnership of feeling
How does Lewis distinguish love from being in love?|Being in love is a feeling that can begin marriage, while love is a maintained commitment and action
Why does Lewis defend permanence in marriage?|The bodily and spiritual union of marriage is meant to be faithful even when feelings fluctuate
What does forgiveness require, according to Lewis?|Hating the evil while willing the good of the person who did it
Why does Lewis say forgiving enemies does not mean approving their actions?|We already hate our own sins while still seeking our own good, and must learn to treat others similarly
What does Lewis call the great sin?|Pride
Why is pride the great sin in Lewis view?|It is competition against God and other people, and it turns the self into the center
Why is pride especially dangerous in religious people?|It can use religion itself as material for superiority
What is the opposite of pride in Lewis account?|Humility, which is not self hatred but freedom from self importance
What is charity in Lewis account?|Christian love that wills the good of another person, whether or not affection is felt
How does Lewis say charity can grow when feelings are absent?|Act lovingly, and affection often follows the obedience
What does Lewis say hatred does to a person?|It trains the heart deeper into hostility, making the person more hateful
What is Christian hope in Lewis account?|Desiring the promised life with God beyond this world while still doing earthly duties well
Why does Lewis say aiming at heaven helps earthly life?|People most useful in this world often acted from a hope beyond it
What error does Lewis identify in disappointed earthly longings?|We blame the object or keep chasing new objects instead of seeing that finite goods point beyond themselves
What is faith in the first sense Lewis describes?|Holding to beliefs reason has accepted when emotions and moods fluctuate
How does Lewis say faith is trained in the first sense?|By regular practices that remind the mind of what it has already judged true
What is faith in the deeper sense at the end of Book III?|Trust in Christ after discovering that one cannot make oneself righteous
Why must a person first try seriously to be good before understanding this deeper faith?|Real effort exposes the depth of dependence on grace
How does Lewis relate faith and good works?|Works are not a bribe to earn salvation, but the natural result of trusting and receiving Christ
Why does Lewis reject both earning salvation and passive inaction?|Christian life is all gift and all response, because God acts in us while we actively obey
What distinction opens Book IV?|Making creates something different from the maker, while begetting produces something of the same kind
How does Lewis use making and begetting to explain divine sonship?|God makes creatures, but the eternal Son is begotten as the same divine kind as the Father
What are Bios and Zoe in Lewis vocabulary?|Bios is natural biological life, while Zoe is the uncreated divine life
What is the Christian goal regarding Bios and Zoe?|Natural life is to be transformed by participation in divine life
Why does Lewis say the Trinity is beyond ordinary personality?|God is not less than personal but more than personal, a three personal life beyond human categories
How does prayer involve the Trinity in Lewis explanation?|The believer prays to the Father, through the Son, by the Spirit working within
Why does Lewis say theology is practical?|Knowing who God is changes how a person prays, trusts, and receives divine life
How does Lewis explain the relation of God to time?|All moments are present to God, so divine attention is not divided by sequence as ours is
Why does the relation of God to time matter for prayer?|God can attend fully to each prayer without needing time to move from one person to another
What is the good infection?|The life of Christ spreading into people by contact with him and with his body
Why does Lewis compare humanity to toy soldiers becoming alive?|Humans are natural creatures who need the divine life to make them true sons of God
Why was becoming sons painful after the fall?|Humanity turned away from God, so transformation now means surrendering a resistant self
What balance does Lewis keep in Two Notes about individuality and unity?|People are organs in one body, so they belong to one another without becoming interchangeable
What two opposite errors does Lewis warn against in Two Notes?|Individualism that denies shared life, and totalitarianism that crushes personal difference
What does Let us pretend mean in Book IV?|Acting as children of God because God commands it, so the practice becomes real transformation
Why can good pretending become real in Lewis argument?|Acting from the role one is called into trains the person and opens the self to Christ
What does Lewis say happens when a person tries to put on Christ?|The person begins noticing thoughts and actions that cannot remain if Christ is truly shaping them
Why is Christianity both hard and easy in Lewis account?|It is hard because Christ asks for the whole self, and easy because surrender is less impossible than keeping the old self and trying to be good
What does Lewis say Christ wants from a person?|Not a portion of time, money, or work, but the whole self
What morning practice does Lewis recommend in the Christian life?|Begin the day by pushing back natural demands and letting the life of Christ set the direction
What does Counting the Cost say about perfection?|Christ will accept nothing less than making the person perfect, though he delights in the first small steps
Why does Lewis compare God to a dentist or builder?|God does not only relieve obvious pain but repairs the whole person into what he intends
What is the living house image about?|A person expects minor repairs, but God is building a palace fit for himself to inhabit
Why are nice people not automatically new people?|Natural niceness can be a gift of temperament, while new life requires surrender to God
What question matters more than comparing nice non-Christians and difficult Christians?|Whether each person is becoming better than they would be without Christ
Why can natural niceness become spiritually dangerous?|It can make a person content with self and unaware of the need for grace
What does Lewis mean by new men?|People transformed by Christ into a new kind of humanity, not merely improved versions of the old kind
How does the new step differ from ordinary evolution in Lewis picture?|It comes from outside nature, is received voluntarily, and changes creatures into sons of God
Why are the new men not all identical?|Christ brings out each true personality instead of flattening differences
How does Lewis explain finding the true self?|A person finds the real self by giving the self to Christ, not by chasing self expression
What is the final paradox of Mere Christianity?|Lose the self to Christ and receive true life, while keeping the self leads to decay and loss
